# Gmail Manage Subscriptions Not Showing? 8 Fixes

Google added a **Manage subscriptions** page to Gmail in July 2025: one list of the senders that email you most, with an **Unsubscribe** button next to each. It is useful, and it is also the source of a very common complaint, because a lot of people open Gmail, look for it, and it is not there.

Here is why it goes missing, in the order worth checking, and two ways to get the same result today if you do not want to wait.

## Where it is supposed to be

**On the web:** open Gmail, look at the left sidebar, and click **More** to expand the list. **Manage subscriptions** appears in that expanded section, alongside Spam, Trash, and Scheduled. It is not in the top part of the sidebar with Inbox and Starred, which is where most people look.

**In the Android and iPhone apps:** tap the **three-line menu** at the top left and scroll down. Manage subscriptions is in the list below your labels.

If it is there, you are done. If not, work down this list.

## Fix 1: Expand the sidebar and click More

This solves it for a surprising number of people. On the web, the sidebar hides everything below Drafts behind a **More** link. If your sidebar is collapsed to icons only (the three-line menu at the top left toggles this), expand it first, then click More.

## Fix 2: Update the Gmail app

The feature reached the web first, then Android and iPhone a couple of weeks later. Older app builds do not have the menu entry at all. Open the App Store or Play Store, update Gmail, force close it, and reopen. Then check the three-line menu again.

## Fix 3: Accept that the rollout may not have reached you

Google's own help page says the feature is "gradually rolling out and may not be available to you yet." That is the only official reason Google gives, and in practice it is the most common one. Rollouts of this kind happen account by account over weeks or months, so a friend having it proves nothing about your account. There is no setting to turn on and no way to request it early.

## Fix 4: Check whether it is a work or school account

Personal Google accounts got Manage subscriptions first. Work and school accounts (Google Workspace) received it later, and organizations get Gmail features on different schedules depending on how their admin has configured release tracks. If you have both kinds of account, sign into the personal one and look. If the feature is there for personal but not work, that is the whole explanation.

## Fix 5: Use Gmail itself, not a mail app

Manage subscriptions is part of Gmail's own interface. If you read Gmail through Outlook, Apple Mail, Thunderbird, Spark, or any other client, you will never see it there. Open mail.google.com in a browser or the official Gmail app.

## Fix 6: Turn off Basic HTML view

If Gmail looks plain and old-fashioned, you may be in the basic HTML view, which lacks most newer features. Look for a link at the bottom of the page that says something like "Switch to standard view" or open mail.google.com/mail/?ui=2 to force the standard interface.

## Fix 7: Check your Gmail language setting

New Gmail features usually ship in English first and reach other display languages later. If Gmail is set to a language other than English, open Settings, then **See all settings**, then **General**, and check the **Language** setting. Switching to English (US) temporarily can reveal features that have not been localized yet.

## Fix 8: Understand what it will not show even when it works

Manage subscriptions lists senders Gmail recognizes as bulk or subscription senders, with a count of their recent emails. It does not show:

- Senders that do not use standard mailing list headers
- Subscriptions that have gone quiet (it focuses on recent volume)
- Anything Gmail already routes to Spam, where it deliberately avoids offering an unsubscribe

It also only unsubscribes. The years of old emails from that sender stay in your inbox until you delete them. If you were hoping to use it to see everything that emails you, it was never going to do that, so the two methods below are worth knowing regardless.

## What to do instead, today

### Method A: the unsubscribe search

Almost every newsletter and marketing email contains the word "unsubscribe." So:

1. Type `unsubscribe` into the Gmail search bar and press Enter.
2. The results are, in effect, your subscription list.
3. Open any of them. At the top, next to the sender name, Gmail shows an **Unsubscribe** link. Click it.

To see only what arrived recently, add a date: `unsubscribe newer_than:30d`. To see the old, dead-weight subscriptions: `unsubscribe older_than:6m is:unread`.

This works on every account type, every region, and every app version. Its limit is that you unsubscribe one sender at a time, and it does not tell you which senders are the biggest.

### Method B: a sender-ranked list

[MailMop](/) does the part Manage subscriptions is trying to do, without waiting for a rollout. It analyzes your inbox in your browser and returns every sender ranked by how many emails they have sent you, with an **Unsubscribe** button next to each, plus **Delete** for clearing the old emails and **Block** for senders that ignore unsubscribe requests. It finds the unsubscribe link inside the email body, not just the list header, which matters for the senders that keep coming after you click Gmail's button.

Your email is processed in your browser and never uploaded. The [code is open source](https://github.com/neilbhammar/mailmop) and the app passed Google's CASA security assessment. There is a free tier.

For the full comparison, see [Gmail's Manage subscriptions vs MailMop](/blog/gmail-manage-subscriptions-vs-mailmop), and for a walkthrough of the feature itself once you have it, [how to use Gmail's Manage subscriptions](/blog/gmail-manage-subscriptions).

## Quick checklist

1. Sidebar expanded, **More** clicked
2. Gmail app updated and restarted
3. Checked a personal account, not only work or school
4. Using mail.google.com or the Gmail app, not a third-party client
5. Standard view, not basic HTML
6. Language set to English (US)
7. Still missing: rollout has not reached you, use the `unsubscribe` search or a sender-ranked tool in the meantime

If none of those apply and it is still missing, it is the rollout. Check back in a few weeks, and clean up by sender in the meantime so there is less to unsubscribe from when it arrives.
