# How to Mass Delete Emails in Gmail (2026)

**Quick answer:** Run a search in Gmail (like `category:promotions` or `from:sender@example.com`), click the **select-all checkbox** at the top of the list, then click the **"Select all conversations that match this search"** link that appears, and hit the trash icon. That deletes every matching email, whether it is 200 or 20,000. Then **empty Trash** to actually free the storage. This only works in Gmail on a desktop browser, not the phone app.

Below: the exact steps, the searches that catch the most junk, how to delete by sender, how to wipe everything, what happens after you delete, and how to do it without losing anything you care about.

## The one link that makes mass deletion work

The select-all checkbox at the top of Gmail only selects the emails on the current page, which is 50 by default (100 at most). People click it, delete, and wonder why 40,000 emails are still there.

The trick is the banner that appears right after you tick that box:

> Select all conversations that match this search

Click it and Gmail selects everything matching your search, across every page. Now a single trash click deletes all of it.

So the full sequence is:

1. Type a search into the Gmail search bar (examples below) and press Enter.
2. Click the **checkbox** at the top left of the message list.
3. Click **"Select all conversations that match this search."**
4. Click the **trash icon**. If Gmail asks you to confirm a bulk action, confirm.

Gmail processes large deletions in the background. For very large sets (tens of thousands), the count may take a minute or two to catch up. Do not refresh mid-delete.

## The searches that clear the most email

Paste these into the search bar. Each one can remove thousands of emails in one pass.

| Goal | Search |
|---|---|
| Promotions older than a month | `category:promotions older_than:1m` |
| Social notifications | `category:social older_than:3m` |
| App and service updates | `category:updates older_than:1y` |
| Forum and mailing list mail | `category:forums older_than:6m` |
| Everything from one sender | `from:newsletter@example.com` |
| Everything from a whole domain | `from:@example.com` |
| Anything you never opened, older than a year | `is:unread older_than:1y` |
| Old email in general | `older_than:2y` |
| Large emails eating storage | `larger:10M` |
| A date range | `after:2019/01/01 before:2021/12/31` |

Protect what matters by adding exclusions. `-is:starred` skips anything you starred. `-from:mybank.com` keeps one sender out of the sweep. `-label:keep` skips anything you labeled "keep." For example:

```
category:promotions older_than:1m -is:starred -label:keep
```

A good habit before any big deletion: open the search, scroll the first page, and make sure nothing personal is in it. Gmail's category detection is good but not perfect.

## Delete by sender (the fast, safe way)

Category searches catch a lot, but the biggest single wins come from individual senders. A job board that emailed you daily since 2021, a store you bought from once, a notification address from an app you deleted years ago. In MailMop's usage data across 16 million analyzed emails, about 1 in 7 of the senders people delete has more than 1,000 emails on its own.

Deleting by sender is also the safest method, because you can see exactly who you are removing. A keyword search might catch a personal email that happens to mention "sale." A sender search cannot.

**Manually:** search `from:` followed by the address (Gmail suggests addresses as you type), select all matching conversations, delete. Repeat for the next sender.

## Delete everything and start fresh

If you want a genuinely empty account:

1. **Back up first.** Go to [Google Takeout](https://takeout.google.com), select Mail, and export. There is no recovery once Trash is emptied. Our [Google Takeout guide](/blog/google-takeout-inbox-analysis) walks through it.
2. Search `in:anywhere` (or click **All Mail** in the sidebar).
3. Checkbox, **Select all conversations that match this search**, trash.
4. Open **Drafts** and **Spam** separately and clear those. All Mail does not include them.
5. Empty **Trash**.

A note on what All Mail covers: it includes your inbox, everything you archived, and everything you sent. Labels and filters survive a full deletion, so your setup for future mail stays intact.

## What actually happens when you delete

- **Deleted email goes to Trash**, not away. It still counts against your 15GB of Google storage.
- **You have 30 days** to change your mind. Open Trash, select the emails, click **Move to Inbox**.
- **After 30 days Gmail empties Trash automatically** and the storage is reclaimed. To get it back immediately, open Trash and click **Empty Trash now**.
- **The storage meter can lag** up to 24 hours after a big deletion. If it has not moved, check again tomorrow.

## Archive instead, if you are not sure

If you cannot decide whether to keep something, archive it. Same steps as deletion, but click the **archive icon** (a box with a down arrow) instead of the trash. Archived mail leaves the inbox, stays fully searchable, and still counts toward storage. It is the right choice for old receipts, account emails, and anything you might need once in the next few years. It is the wrong choice if the goal is freeing space.

## Mass deletion mistakes to avoid

- **Deleting in the phone app.** It has no "select all matching" option. Use a desktop browser, or a sender-based tool that works on mobile.
- **Skipping the "Select all conversations that match" link.** You will delete one page and think you are done.
- **Forgetting to empty Trash.** No storage is freed until you do.
- **Keyword-deleting personal mail.** Prefer senders and categories, and always skim the first page of results.
- **Deleting instead of unsubscribing.** The newsletter comes back tomorrow. Unsubscribe from the ones you never read. See [how to unsubscribe from emails in Gmail](/blog/how-to-unsubscribe-gmail-2026).
- **Not backing up before a full wipe.** Takeout takes ten minutes and prevents a very bad week.

## Troubleshooting

**The "Select all conversations" link does not appear.** It only shows after you tick the top checkbox and only when the search has more results than fit on one page. If the search returned fewer than a page, everything is already selected.

**Deletion seems stuck.** Gmail processes very large deletions in batches. Leave the tab open and give it a few minutes. Refreshing does not speed it up.

**Storage number has not changed.** Empty Trash, then wait up to 24 hours for the meter to update.

**I deleted something important.** Open Trash within 30 days and move it back to the inbox.
