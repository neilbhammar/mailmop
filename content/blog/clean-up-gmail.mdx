# How to Clean Up Gmail: The Complete 2026 Guide

A cluttered Gmail inbox buries the mail that matters, eats the 15GB of storage your whole Google account shares, and makes every search slower. The fix is not deleting emails one at a time. Almost nobody finishes that.

This guide is built around the one idea that makes cleanup fast: **clean by sender, not by email.** A few dozen senders cause most of the mess. Deal with them and tens of thousands of emails disappear in minutes.

It covers the free manual method (Gmail's own search and filters), the automatic method (a tool that groups your inbox by sender), and how to keep the inbox clean afterward so you only do this once.

## Quick start: clean up Gmail in 10 minutes

If you only have ten minutes, do these in order:

1. **Empty Spam and Trash.** Both count against your storage.
2. Search `category:promotions older_than:1m`, click the select-all box, click **"Select all conversations that match this search,"** delete.
3. Search `larger:10M` and delete the big attachments you do not need. This is where your storage is.
4. Pick your three worst senders (the newsletter you never open, the retailer, the job board). Search `from:` each one, select all matching, delete.
5. **Empty Trash again.** Nothing is freed until you do.

That routine clears the bulk of most inboxes. The rest of this guide is the thorough version.

## Before you start: protect what matters

Three minutes of prep prevents the one mistake people regret.

- **Star or label anything you cannot lose.** Receipts, legal mail, account recovery emails. Apply a label called "Keep" and you can exclude it from every deletion search with `-label:keep`.
- **Archive, do not delete, when unsure.** Archived mail leaves the inbox but stays in All Mail and stays searchable.
- **Know the undo window.** Deleted email sits in Trash for 30 days. You can move it back to the inbox from there. After Trash is emptied, it is gone.
- **Back up if you are doing a full wipe.** [Google Takeout](https://takeout.google.com) exports everything in about ten minutes. Our [Takeout guide](/blog/google-takeout-inbox-analysis) covers it.

## The one Gmail trick that changes everything

When you tick the checkbox at the top of a Gmail search, it selects only the emails on that page (50 by default). A banner then appears above the list:

> Select all conversations that match this search

Click it, and Gmail selects every matching email across every page. Now a single trash click deletes all of them, whether that is 300 or 30,000. Every method below depends on this link. If a search returns 12,000 results and you only deleted 50, you missed it.

## Method 1: Clean up Gmail manually (free)

### Step 1: Empty Spam and Trash

Open **Spam** and click **Delete all spam messages now**. Open **Trash** and click **Empty Trash now**. Both empty themselves after 30 days, but whatever is in them right now still counts toward your storage.

### Step 2: Bulk delete by category

Gmail already sorts incoming mail into Promotions, Social, Updates, and Forums. Those categories work as search filters, which makes them the fastest way to remove low value email in bulk.

| Search | Clears |
|---|---|
| `category:promotions older_than:1m` | Marketing email older than a month |
| `category:social older_than:3m` | Social network notifications |
| `category:updates older_than:1y` | Receipts, confirmations, app notifications older than a year |
| `category:forums older_than:6m` | Mailing lists and forum digests |

For each: search, tick the box, click **Select all conversations that match**, delete. Add `-is:starred` to skip starred mail and `-label:keep` to skip your Keep label.

Skim the first page of results before deleting. Gmail's categorization is good but not perfect, and a personal email occasionally lands in Updates.

### Step 3: Delete large attachments

This step is about storage rather than clutter, but it is where most of your 15GB actually went. A normal email is under 100KB. A video someone sent you in 2019 is 20MB.

| Search | Finds |
|---|---|
| `larger:25M` | The very largest emails |
| `larger:10M` | The next tier, usually a few hundred |
| `filename:mp4 OR filename:mov OR filename:zip` | Videos and archives |
| `in:sent larger:5M` | Big things you sent (Sent counts too) |

Download anything you need first, then delete. There is a fuller version of this in [Gmail storage full? How to free up space](/blog/gmail-storage-full).

### Step 4: Delete by sender

Categories catch the obvious stuff. The biggest individual wins come from specific senders that have been piling up for years.

Search `from:` and the address (Gmail autocompletes it), select all matching, delete. To clear a whole company at once, use the domain: `from:@example.com`.

The hard part is knowing which senders to search for, because Gmail has no "who emails me most" view. Two ways to find them:

- Scroll your Promotions and Updates tabs and note which names keep appearing.
- Search `is:unread older_than:6m` and look at the sender column. Senders you never open are senders you should clear.

There is a dedicated walkthrough in [how to delete all emails from one sender](/blog/delete-emails-from-one-sender-gmail).

### Step 5: Unsubscribe from what you never read

Deleting today's newsletter does nothing about tomorrow's. For any sender you never open, unsubscribe so it stops arriving.

Gmail shows an **Unsubscribe** link next to the sender name at the top of most marketing emails. Click it and Gmail either sends the unsubscribe request for you or opens the sender's page. Gmail also has a **Manage subscriptions** view (in the left sidebar under More) that lists senders with the most recent mail and lets you unsubscribe from each. It is useful but limited to recent senders and one click per sender. See [how Gmail's Manage subscriptions works](/blog/gmail-manage-subscriptions) and [how to unsubscribe from emails in Gmail](/blog/how-to-unsubscribe-gmail-2026) for the faster methods.

### Step 6: Deal with what is left

After the bulk passes, what remains is usually a few thousand emails that do not fit a category. Two options:

- **Archive everything older than a year.** Search `older_than:1y`, select all matching, click the archive icon. The inbox is clean, nothing is lost, and search still finds all of it.
- **Delete everything older than a year that you never opened.** `is:unread older_than:1y` is a surprisingly safe deletion. If you did not open it in a year, you will not.

### Step 7: Empty Trash

Everything you deleted is in Trash, still counting. Open **Trash**, **Empty Trash now**. The storage meter at [one.google.com/storage](https://one.google.com/storage) can take up to 24 hours to update.

## Cleaning up email that is not Gmail

The sender-first idea works everywhere, the mechanics differ:

- **Outlook.com** has a **Sweep** button that deletes all mail from a sender and can keep doing it for future mail. Search a sender, select a message, click Sweep.
- **Yahoo Mail** lets you sort by sender in the message list and has a bulk unsubscribe view under Settings, then Subscriptions.
- **iCloud Mail** has no bulk-by-sender tools. Use the search, select all, delete, and lean on Mail rules for the future.

## How to keep Gmail clean

The real goal is an inbox that does not need another cleanup in six months.

### Set up filters that act on arrival

Settings, then **Filters and Blocked Addresses**, then **Create a new filter**. Three filters cover most people:

1. **Receipts and confirmations skip the inbox.** Criteria: `subject:(receipt OR confirmation OR "your order")`. Action: Skip the Inbox, apply label "Receipts."
2. **Known senders you keep for reference get archived.** Criteria: `from:@utility-company.com`. Action: Skip the Inbox, apply label.
3. **Senders you never want get deleted.** Criteria: `from:` the address. Action: Delete it. (Blocking a sender in Gmail does the same thing and is one click from any of their emails: open the three-dot menu, **Block**.)

### Use the tabs

If you turned off Gmail's Promotions and Social tabs, turn them back on (Settings, **Inbox**, **Categories**). They keep marketing and notifications out of your primary view and make the monthly cleanup searches above much cleaner.

### A maintenance routine that takes minutes

- **Weekly, one minute:** `category:promotions older_than:1m`, select all, delete, empty Trash.
- **Monthly, ten minutes:** unsubscribe from anything you did not open this month, `larger:10M` for new attachments.
- **Twice a year:** rerun a sender analysis and clear whatever has grown.

### Unsubscribe the moment you stop reading

Every newsletter you unsubscribe from is clutter that never comes back. When you catch yourself deleting the same sender for the third time, unsubscribe instead.

## Mistakes that make Gmail cleanup take forever

- **Deleting one email at a time.** Always work by search, category, or sender.
- **Skipping the "Select all conversations that match" link.** You deleted one page, not the inbox.
- **Forgetting Trash.** No storage is freed until it is emptied.
- **Cleaning in the phone app.** It has no select-all-matching option. Use a desktop browser or a sender-based tool.
- **Deleting instead of unsubscribing.** Same senders, same inbox, next month.
- **Ignoring Drive and Photos.** They share the same 15GB. If storage was the problem, check the breakdown at one.google.com/storage before assuming it is Gmail.
